CARDIAC ARREST AND RESUSCITATION - EMERGENCY TRIAGE PROTOCOL
=============================================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from ERC/AHA Resuscitation Guidelines 2021

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Cardiac arrest is the sudden cessation of cardiac mechanical activity confirmed by
unresponsiveness, absent breathing (or only gasping), and absence of detectable pulse.
Every minute without CPR reduces survival by 7-10%. Brain damage begins at 4 minutes.
Most survivable cardiac arrests are in shockable rhythms: VF and pulseless VT.

2. RECOGNITION AND RED FLAGS
------------------------------
  - Unresponsive patient who cannot be roused
  - No normal breathing (no breathing, agonal/gasping only)
  - No pulse detected (check carotid for 10 seconds maximum)
  - Witnessed collapse in public or at home
  - AED delivered shock before arrival (prehospital shockable rhythm)
  - Patient found down with unknown downtime
  - Syncope that did not resolve (sustained loss of consciousness)

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red) — ACTIVATE RESUSCITATION TEAM IMMEDIATELY:
  Criteria: No pulse + no breathing. ANY unresponsive patient.
  Action:
    - Call resuscitation team IMMEDIATELY
    - Begin CPR: 30 compressions : 2 breaths (or continuous compressions if no airway)
    - Compression rate: 100-120/min, depth: 5-6 cm, full chest recoil
    - Attach AED/defibrillator as soon as available
    - If VF/pulseless VT: defibrillate immediately (360J monophasic, 200J biphasic)
    - IV/IO access, adrenaline 1mg IV every 3-5 minutes
    - Identify and treat reversible causes (4H/4T)
  Timeframe: No delay — every second counts

4. REVERSIBLE CAUSES (4H / 4T)
--------------------------------
  4H: Hypoxia, Hypovolaemia, Hypo/Hyperkalaemia, Hypothermia
  4T: Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade, Toxins/Overdose, Thrombosis (PE or coronary)

5. POST-RESUSCITATION CARE (ROSC)
-----------------------------------
  - Targeted Temperature Management: 32-36°C for 24 hours if comatose
  - 12-lead ECG immediately (STEMI → activate cath lab)
  - CT head if neurological cause suspected
  - Continuous monitoring: SpO2, ETCO2, BP, glucose
  - Avoid hypoxia (target SpO2 94-98%) and hyperoxia

6. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS FOR WITNESSED COLLAPSE
----------------------------------------------------
  Q1: Was the collapse witnessed? How long ago?
  Q2: Did the patient complain of chest pain or palpitations before collapsing?
  Q3: Was there any seizure activity?
  Q4: Does the patient have known heart disease, arrhythmia, or cardiomyopathy?
  Q5: Any medications — especially antiarrhythmics, digoxin, QT-prolonging drugs?
  Q6: Any recent drug or alcohol use? (toxicological cause)
  Q7: Was an AED used? How many shocks? Any ROSC?

7. SPECIAL CONSIDERATIONS
---------------------------
  Drowning: Hypothermia is protective — extend resuscitation. Core temperature must be
  assessed before declaring death (rewarming resuscitation).
  Pregnancy: Left lateral tilt. Manual uterine displacement during CPR. Consider perimortem C-section at 4 minutes.
  Paediatric: Use weight-based dosing. Airway management is first priority (hypoxic arrest).
  Trauma: Control haemorrhage first. Tension pneumothorax must be decompressed before CPR is effective.
