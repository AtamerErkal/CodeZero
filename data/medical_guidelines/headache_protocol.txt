HEADACHE — EMERGENCY TRIAGE PROTOCOL
======================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from ACEP Clinical Policies, EFNS/EHF Guidelines 2023

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Headache is one of the most frequent ED presentations. While most headaches are
benign (migraine, tension-type), a small but critical proportion represent serious
pathology. The "worst headache of my life" (thunderclap) must be treated as
subarachnoid haemorrhage until proven otherwise.

2. RED FLAGS — IMMEDIATE DANGER ("SNOOPV" Criteria)
-----------------------------------------------------
  - S: Systemic symptoms (fever, neck stiffness, rash) — meningitis / encephalitis
  - N: Neurological symptoms (focal deficit, confusion, diplopia, speech problems)
  - O: Onset sudden / "thunderclap" (peak severity in <1 minute) — subarachnoid
  - O: Onset after age 50 (new headache in older adult — giant cell arteritis, tumour)
  - P: Positional headache (worse lying down — raised ICP; better standing — low pressure)
  - V: Vomiting at peak headache intensity (subarachnoid, raised ICP)
  Additional red flags:
  - Headache triggered by exertion, sex, or Valsalva (coital headache, subarachnoid)
  - Immunocompromised patient with new headache (opportunistic infection)
  - HIV-positive patient with headache (cryptococcal meningitis)
  - Headache with papilloedema on fundoscopy (raised ICP)

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red):
  Criteria: Thunderclap onset, OR fever + neck stiffness, OR focal neuro deficit
  Action: CT head (non-contrast) STAT. If CT negative + thunderclap → LP. Neurological assessment.
  Timeframe: Immediate
  Suspected: Subarachnoid haemorrhage, meningitis, encephalitis, stroke, epidural haematoma

LEVEL 2 - URGENT (Orange):
  Criteria: New headache pattern, >50 years, or any SNOOPV criteria without shock
  Action: Neurological exam, CT head within 1 hour, full history
  Timeframe: Assessment within 30 minutes
  Suspected: Temporal arteritis, intracranial hypertension, subdural haematoma

LEVEL 3 - ROUTINE (Green):
  Criteria: Known migraine/tension pattern, NO red flags, prior similar episodes
  Action: Pain relief protocol, migraine cocktail, antiemetics
  Timeframe: Assessment within 2 hours
  Suspected: Migraine, tension-type headache, cluster headache

4. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS
----------------------------
  Q1: Did this headache come on very suddenly, reaching maximum pain within seconds? (thunderclap)
  Q2: Do you have a fever, stiff neck, or light sensitivity? (meningitis triad)
  Q3: Is this the worst headache you have ever had? (subarachnoid alarm)
  Q4: Do you have any weakness, numbness, vision changes, or speech problems? (focal deficit)
  Q5: Where exactly is the pain? (temporal = arteritis, occipital = posterior fossa)
  Q6: Have you had headaches like this before?
  Q7: Did the headache start during or after physical exertion or sexual activity?

5. MIGRAINE MANAGEMENT (Routine)
----------------------------------
  First line: Sumatriptan 6mg SC or 100mg PO, metoclopramide 10mg IV, aspirin 1g PO
  Second line: Prochlorperazine 10mg IV, dexamethasone 10mg IV (reduces recurrence)
  Hydration: 1L normal saline if dehydrated
  Contraindication: Triptans contraindicated in hemiplegic migraine, basilar migraine

6. THUNDERCLAP HEADACHE WORKUP
--------------------------------
  Step 1: CT head without contrast (90% sensitive for SAH within 6h of onset)
  Step 2: If CT negative → LP at 12h post onset (xanthochromia)
  Step 3: CT angiography if LP positive or high suspicion
