POISONING AND DRUG OVERDOSE - TRIAGE PROTOCOL
===============================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from TOXBASE, WHO Poisoning Management Guidelines 2023

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Poisoning and overdose are common emergency presentations. The clinical syndrome
("toxidrome") often identifies the responsible substance class before laboratory
confirmation. Time is critical for antidote-responsive poisons (paracetamol, opioids,
organophosphates). Always consider intentional overdose in a mental health context.

2. RED FLAGS — IMMEDIATE DANGER
---------------------------------
  - Decreased or rapidly decreasing level of consciousness (GCS <13)
  - Respiratory depression (<12 breaths/min) or apnoea (opioid toxidrome)
  - Seizures (tricyclics, isoniazid, cocaine, carbon monoxide, organophosphates)
  - Hypotension or haemodynamic instability
  - Cardiac arrhythmias (QRS widening — TCAs; QT prolongation — antipsychotics)
  - Metabolic acidosis with high anion gap (methanol, ethylene glycol, salicylates, CO)
  - Hyperthermia >40°C (serotonin syndrome, sympathomimetics, NMS)
  - Ingestion of unknown substance in child

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red):
  Criteria: Altered consciousness, respiratory failure, seizures, cardiovascular compromise
  Action: ABCDE. IV access. Monitor. Specific antidotes as appropriate (see below).
  Timeframe: Immediate
  Suspected: Opioid overdose, TCA overdose, CO poisoning, organophosphate

LEVEL 2 - URGENT (Orange):
  Criteria: Symptomatic, haemodynamically stable, potential toxicity risk (paracetamol overdose within 4h)
  Action: Bloods (paracetamol level at 4h post ingestion, LFT, INR, creatinine, ECG)
  Timeframe: Assessment within 30 minutes
  Suspected: Paracetamol overdose, benzodiazepine overdose, alcohol poisoning

LEVEL 3 - ROUTINE (Green):
  Criteria: Asymptomatic, low toxicity substance, >4h post-ingestion, small/unknown dose
  Action: Poison control consultation, observation, bloods if indicated
  Timeframe: Assessment within 2 hours
  Suspected: Non-toxic ingestion, asymptomatic minor overdose

4. TOXIDROMES — CLINICAL RECOGNITION
--------------------------------------
  Opioid: Miosis, respiratory depression, coma, bradycardia | Antidote: Naloxone 0.4-2mg IV/IM/IN
  Sympathomimetic: Mydriasis, tachycardia, hypertension, diaphoresis, agitation | Benzodiazepines
  Anticholinergic: Dry skin, mydriasis, tachycardia, urinary retention, hyperthermia, delirium
  Cholinergic: SLUDGE (salivation, lacrimation, urination, defecation, GI cramps, emesis) + bradycardia | Atropine
  Serotonin Syndrome: Clonus, hyperreflexia, agitation, hyperthermia, diaphoresis | Cyproheptadine
  TCA Toxicity: QRS widening, hypotension, seizures | Sodium bicarbonate
  CO Poisoning: Headache, cherry-red skin, altered consciousness | 100% oxygen

5. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS
----------------------------
  Q1: What did you take? How much? When? (substance, dose, timing)
  Q2: Did you take it intentionally or accidentally?
  Q3: Did you take alcohol or other substances at the same time?
  Q4: Do you feel very sleepy, confused, or is it hard to stay awake?
  Q5: Are you having difficulty breathing?
  Q6: Do you have any chest pains or irregular heartbeat?
  Q7: Have you vomited? (decontamination status)

6. PARACETAMOL (ACETAMINOPHEN) OVERDOSE
-----------------------------------------
  Threshold: >150mg/kg or >7.5g in adults is potentially toxic
  Key window: Activated charcoal if <1 hour post ingestion
  Antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — start if Rumack-Matthew nomogram indicates risk
  Paracetamol level: Measured at 4 hours post ingestion (earlier unreliable)
  LFTs: May be normal for 24-48h then rise sharply — do not falsely reassure

7. DECONTAMINATION
-------------------
  Activated charcoal: Only if conscious, able to protect airway, within 1h of ingestion
  Do NOT induce vomiting: Risks aspiration, no evidence of benefit
  Gastric lavage: Rarely used — only for massive ingestion of immediately life-threatening substance
  Whole bowel irrigation: For sustained-release preparations, body packing
