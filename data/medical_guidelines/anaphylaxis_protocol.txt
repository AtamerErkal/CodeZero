ANAPHYLAXIS - EMERGENCY TRIAGE PROTOCOL
========================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from WAO Anaphylaxis Guidelines 2023

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Anaphylaxis is a severe, life-threatening systemic hypersensitivity reaction. It can
develop within seconds to minutes after exposure to a trigger. Epinephrine
(adrenaline) is the first-line treatment. Any delay increases mortality risk.
Common triggers: foods (nuts, shellfish, milk), medications (penicillin, NSAIDs),
insect stings (bee, wasp), latex.

2. RED FLAGS (Requires Immediate Emergency Triage)
---------------------------------------------------
  - Throat swelling, stridor, difficulty swallowing (angioedema of airway)
  - Severe difficulty breathing, wheezing (bronchospasm)
  - Hypotension (systolic <90 mmHg), dizziness, loss of consciousness
  - Rapidly spreading urticaria or angioedema after known allergen exposure
  - Abdominal cramps, vomiting after allergen with skin or respiratory symptoms
  - History of prior severe allergic reaction (epi-pen user)
  - Pale, diaphoretic, clammy skin (circulatory collapse)

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red):
  Criteria: Airway compromise, OR circulatory collapse, OR severe respiratory distress
  Action: Epinephrine 0.5mg IM (lateral thigh) IMMEDIATELY. IV access. Lie flat, legs elevated.
  Timeframe: Immediate — no delay acceptable
  Suspected: Anaphylactic shock, airway anaphylaxis

LEVEL 2 - URGENT (Orange):
  Criteria: Generalised urticaria + any systemic symptom (GI, mild wheeze, mild hypotension)
  Action: Epinephrine IM, antihistamine IV, monitor closely. IV access.
  Timeframe: Assessment within 15 minutes
  Suspected: Moderate anaphylaxis, generalised allergic reaction

LEVEL 3 - ROUTINE (Green):
  Criteria: Isolated urticaria or angioedema, NO systemic symptoms, NO airway involvement
  Action: Antihistamine, monitor for 4 hours (biphasic risk)
  Timeframe: Assessment within 1 hour
  Suspected: Local allergic reaction, isolated urticaria

4. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS
----------------------------
  Q1: What did you come into contact with before symptoms started? (allergen identification)
  Q2: Do you have difficulty breathing or is your throat swelling? (airway)
  Q3: Do you feel dizzy, faint, or is your heart racing? (circulatory)
  Q4: Do you have hives, rash, or skin swelling? Where on the body?
  Q5: Do you have stomach cramps or vomiting?
  Q6: Have you had a severe allergic reaction before? Do you carry an EpiPen?
  Q7: Have you already used your EpiPen?

5. ALLERGEN CATEGORIES
-----------------------
  Foods: Peanuts, tree nuts, shellfish, fish, milk, egg, wheat, sesame
  Medications: Penicillin, aspirin, NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, contrast agents
  Stings: Bee, wasp, hornet, fire ant
  Other: Latex, exercise-induced, cold-induced, idiopathic

6. BIPHASIC REACTION
---------------------
  - 5-20% of anaphylaxis cases have a biphasic reaction (return of symptoms 1-72h later)
  - All patients treated for anaphylaxis must be observed for minimum 4 hours
  - Patients with severe reactions must be observed 6-12 hours
  - Prescribe EpiPen x2 at discharge for all confirmed anaphylaxis patients
