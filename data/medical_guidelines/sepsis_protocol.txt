SEPSIS AND SEPTIC SHOCK - EMERGENCY TRIAGE PROTOCOL
====================================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from Surviving Sepsis Campaign Guidelines 2021

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Sepsis is a life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response
to infection. Septic shock is a subset with circulatory and cellular/metabolic
abnormalities. Early recognition and the "Sepsis 1-Hour Bundle" are critical.
Mortality increases by ~7% for each hour without antibiotics in septic shock.

2. RED FLAGS — SEPSIS CRITERIA (qSOFA + Clinical Suspicion)
------------------------------------------------------------
qSOFA criteria (2+ = high risk):
  - Altered mental status (confusion, agitation, new onset)
  - Respiratory rate ≥22 breaths/minute
  - Systolic BP ≤100 mmHg

Additional red flags:
  - Temperature >38.3°C or <36°C (fever or hypothermia)
  - Heart rate >90 bpm
  - Suspected infection source (pneumonia, UTI, wound, IV line, surgical site)
  - WBC >12,000 or <4,000 cells/μL
  - Lactate >2 mmol/L (tissue hypoperfusion)
  - Mottled skin, prolonged capillary refill >3 seconds
  - Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/hour (oliguria)

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red):
  Criteria: Suspected infection + septic shock (vasopressor requirement OR lactate >2)
  Action: Activate Sepsis Alert. IV access x2. Lactate STAT. Blood cultures x2 (before abx).
          Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour. 30mL/kg crystalloid bolus.
  Timeframe: Immediate. Sepsis 1-Hour Bundle starts now.
  Suspected: Septic shock, severe sepsis

LEVEL 2 - URGENT (Orange):
  Criteria: Suspected infection + 2+ qSOFA OR fever with haemodynamic instability
  Action: Blood cultures, lactate, CBC, CMP, CRP, procalcitonin. IV antibiotics within 3 hours.
  Timeframe: Assessment within 30 minutes
  Suspected: Sepsis (without shock)

LEVEL 3 - ROUTINE (Green):
  Criteria: Infection suspected, NO qSOFA criteria, haemodynamically stable
  Action: Standard workup, oral antibiotics if appropriate
  Timeframe: Assessment within 2 hours
  Suspected: Localised infection, cellulitis, uncomplicated UTI

4. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS
----------------------------
  Q1: Do you have a fever, chills, or feel very cold?
  Q2: Are you confused or not thinking clearly? (to nurse: assess orientation)
  Q3: Is your breathing faster than normal?
  Q4: Do you have a known infection, wound, or have you had recent surgery?
  Q5: Are you urinating less than normal?
  Q6: Do you have a central line, urinary catheter, or other device?
  Q7: Do you have diabetes, cancer, or are you on immunosuppressants?

5. INFECTION SOURCE SCREENING
------------------------------
  Pulmonary: Cough, purulent sputum, infiltrate on CXR
  Urinary: Dysuria, flank pain, cloudy urine, recent catheter
  Intra-abdominal: Abdominal pain, distension, recent surgery, peritonitis signs
  Skin/Soft tissue: Wound, cellulitis, abscess, IV line site erythema
  CNS: Headache, neck stiffness, photophobia (consider meningitis)

6. ONE-HOUR BUNDLE (Surviving Sepsis Campaign)
-----------------------------------------------
  □ Measure lactate level
  □ Obtain blood cultures before antibiotics
  □ Administer broad-spectrum antibiotics
  □ Begin 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L
  □ Apply vasopressors if septic shock persists after fluids
