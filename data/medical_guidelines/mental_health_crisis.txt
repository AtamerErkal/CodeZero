MENTAL HEALTH CRISIS AND PSYCHIATRIC EMERGENCY - TRIAGE PROTOCOL
==================================================================
Version: 1.0 | Last Updated: 2025-11-01
Source: Adapted from NICE CG136, ACEP Psychiatric Guidelines 2023

1. OVERVIEW
-----------
Psychiatric emergencies require rapid assessment of risk (suicide, self-harm, violence),
medical causes of altered behaviour, and appropriate safe environment. Always consider
organic causes first: hypoglycaemia, intoxication, sepsis, head injury, metabolic crisis.
Never assume a presentation is purely psychiatric without a medical evaluation.

2. RED FLAGS — IMMEDIATE RISK
------------------------------
  - Active suicidal ideation with plan and means (intent)
  - Recent deliberate self-harm or suicide attempt (overdose, laceration, hanging)
  - Command hallucinations instructing harm to self or others
  - Violent or agitated patient with risk to self or staff
  - Suspected overdose: altered consciousness, coma, vomiting, bradycardia/tachycardia
  - Acute psychosis with severe agitation or paranoid aggression
  - Catatonia (stupor, rigidity, mutism) — may be life-threatening
  - Neuroleptic malignant syndrome: hyperthermia, rigidity, altered consciousness in antipsychotic user

3. TRIAGE LEVELS
-----------------

LEVEL 1 - EMERGENCY (Red):
  Criteria: Active self-harm/suicide attempt, drug overdose, violent/restrained patient, NMS
  Action: Secure environment. Medical assessment. Resuscitation if needed. 1:1 supervision.
  Timeframe: Immediate
  Suspected: Overdose, serious self-harm, psychotic crisis, NMS

LEVEL 2 - URGENT (Orange):
  Criteria: Active suicidal ideation with plan, acute psychosis, severe agitation
  Action: Safe environment, psychiatric assessment, consider medication (lorazepam, haloperidol)
  Timeframe: Assessment within 30 minutes
  Suspected: Acute psychotic episode, severe mood episode, high-risk suicidal ideation

LEVEL 3 - ROUTINE (Green):
  Criteria: Stable mood crisis, chronic suicidal thoughts (no plan/intent), anxiety
  Action: Mental health liaison, social support assessment, GP referral
  Timeframe: Assessment within 2 hours
  Suspected: Crisis reaction, adjustment disorder, chronic low-risk self-harm

4. KEY ASSESSMENT QUESTIONS
----------------------------
  Q1: Are you having thoughts of hurting yourself or ending your life?
  Q2: Have you harmed yourself today or taken anything? (overdose screening)
  Q3: Do you have a plan? Do you have access to means (medications, weapons)?
  Q4: Have you heard voices or seen things others cannot see?
  Q5: Are you feeling very agitated or unable to control your behaviour?
  Q6: What medications do you take? Have you taken them all? Any substances?
  Q7: Is there someone with you or who can stay with you?

5. RISK STRATIFICATION (Columbia Scale)
-----------------------------------------
  High risk: Active intent, plan, access to means, prior attempt, social isolation
  Moderate risk: Passive ideation, no clear plan, some protective factors
  Low risk: Fleeting thoughts, strong protective factors, good support, engages with help

6. ORGANIC CAUSES TO EXCLUDE
------------------------------
  Metabolic: Hypoglycaemia, uraemia, hepatic encephalopathy, thyroid crisis
  CNS: Head injury, stroke, encephalitis, brain tumour, seizure (postictal)
  Substances: Alcohol intoxication/withdrawal, stimulant intoxication, opioid overdose
  Medications: Serotonin syndrome (SSRI + MAOI), NMS (antipsychotics), steroid psychosis

7. DE-ESCALATION PRINCIPLES
-----------------------------
  - Use calm, non-threatening language. Introduce yourself clearly.
  - Give personal space. Avoid blocking exits (patient safety + staff safety).
  - Listen actively — acknowledge distress without challenging delusions.
  - Offer food/drink when safe — basic needs first.
  - Involve family/support person if consented and safe to do so.
